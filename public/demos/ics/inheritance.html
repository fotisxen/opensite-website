Inheritance & estates

Inherited property in Greece? We handle it, start to finish.

Our lawyers and notaries in Athens accept the inheritance for you, pay the tax and register the property, while you sign a single power of attorney at your nearest ICS office. No trip to Greece needed.

Most inheritance cases we handle start abroad: a parent who lived in Toronto or Montreal, a house in the village, a will written in English. We bring the two systems together, with certified translations, Greek legal work and the tax and land-registry steps, so the property ends up in your name and ready to keep, rent or sell.

What we do

  • Acceptance of inheritance
  • Renunciation of inheritance
  • Recognition of Canadian / foreign wills
  • Publication and validation of wills
  • Division of property between heirs
  • Inheritance tax declaration
  • Land registry and cadastre (Ktimatologio)
  • Estate trustee for property in Greece

How it works

  1. Free first consultationWe look at the family tree, the will and the property, and give you a fixed price.
  2. One power of attorneySigned at your nearest ICS office or Greek consulate.
  3. Documents & translationsDeath certificate, will and family status, translated and certified by us.
  4. Acceptance & taxOur notary signs the acceptance and we file the inheritance tax.
  5. RegistrationLand registry, cadastre and your E9 updated. You get the final documents.

Documents you’ll usually need

📎 Death certificate📎 Will (if any)📎 Family status certificate📎 Passport📎 Greek tax number (AFM)📎 Title deeds, if you have them

Questions we hear often

Do I need to travel to Greece?

No. You sign one power of attorney at an ICS office or a Greek consulate, and our team in Athens does the rest.

Is there a deadline to accept or renounce?

Renunciation has a deadline, which is longer when the heir lives abroad (usually one year). Acceptance has no fixed deadline, but taxes and penalties can build up. Ask us early and we confirm the dates for your case.

What changes with the new inheritance law?

Law 5303/2026 applies to deaths from 16 September 2026. The share of forced heirs becomes a money claim instead of co-ownership, inheritance contracts become possible, and the surviving spouse gets more in some cases. Read our article for details.
